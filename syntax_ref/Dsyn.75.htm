dut clock period (VCL)
Title: DEFINE THE DUT CLOCK
The dut clock period function is used in the Timing section of a VCL test to specify the period of the DUT clock. A VCL test can have only one DUT clock. If the DUT clock and the internal clock are both required in a VCL test, the testhead must have at least two modules because the DUT clock is derived from the internal clock circuit. If a DUT clock is required, the inputs formatted clock function must be used in the Declaration section of the test to specify the pin group which is to drive the clock. The group can have only one pin.
The DUT clock is a continuous square‑wave which is driven to the board under test during the VCL test. The clock continues to run during waits (at event wait function) and pauses (see the pause function).
Syntax
  dut clock period <time>
Parameters
<time>
The period, in seconds, of the DUT clock.
Examples
  ! in the Declaration section
        . . .
     assign Free_Clock to pins 14
        . . .
     inputs formatted clock Free_Clock
        . . .
  ! in the Timing section
        . . .
     dut clock period 110n
        . . .
General Information
The DUT clock is often used as an external clock to synchronize both the board under test and the reference clock. To do this, you drive the DUT clock to the board and then route the clock back through the fixture to the reference clock input. In this way the timing of the DUT clock appears to the test system to be properly synchronized to the board, as if the DUT clock actually were an external clock.
