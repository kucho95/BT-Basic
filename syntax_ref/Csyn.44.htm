connect-unpowered (ANALOG)
Title: CONNECT BUSES TO NODES
The connect-unpowered function is used within an analog in-circuit test block to connect specified buses to specified nodes. This function does not open any relays.
See also the syntax description of the following statements:
• connect-powered (ANALOG)
• disconnect-powered (ANALOG)
• disconnect-unpowered (ANALOG)
• clear connect-powered (ANALOG)
• clear connect-unpowered (ANALOG)
Syntax
  connect <connection>
  connect <connection>;<connection>
  connect <connection>;<connection>; . . . . <connection>
     <connection> can be:   <bus> to <node>
                            <bus> to <node>,<node>
                            <bus> to <node>,<node>, . . . . <node>
        <bus> can be:   s    a
                        i    b
                        g    l
                        gl
        <node> can be a <node id> or a <brc>
           <node id> is a <string constant>
           <brc> is <numeric constant>
 
 
 
You can connect multiple nodes with the g bus only. To make multiple connections with the other buses, you must specify the connections as brc's.
Parameters
 
<node id>
A string constant identifying the node to which the specified bus is to connect. The node name must be the same one entered for that node in the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software.
<brc>
A numeric constant describing the physical location (coordinate) of the module pin that connects to the specified node. A coordinate consists of five numeric characters that are expressed in a brc (Bank Row Column) format:
• The first numeric character (B) designates the testhead bank, either 1 or 2. Bank 1 consists of the two modules on the right side of the testhead, and bank 2 is the two modules on the left side (as viewed from the front of the testhead).
• The next two numeric characters (R) designate the X-axis row, beginning with row 01 at the rear of the testhead and ending with row 23 at the front of the testhead (leading zeros are required). Note that row 12 is unused.
• The last two numeric characters (C) designate the Y-axis column, beginning with column 01 at the right side of the bank and ending with column 78 at the left side of the bank (leading zeros are required).
<bus>
Identifies the bus that is to be connected to the specified node. The G bus is automatically connected to ground in unpowered testing. Note that only the G bus allows connections to more than one node.
Examples
! A simple in-circuit resistor test.
     disconnect all
     connect s to "R1-1"; i to "R1-2"
     connect g to "Node_6", "Node_7"
     resistor 10k, 10, 10
 
! Move the i bus to make a second measurement in the same in-circuit test block.
     disconnect all
     connect s to "R1-1"
     connect i to "R1-2"
     resistor "R1", 5k, 10, 10
     disconnect i from "R1-2"
     connect i to "R2-2"
     resistor "R2", 5k, 10, 10
General Information
Be careful when using brc locations, instead of node names, to avoid multiplexing conflicts.
Note that the G and L buses are the only two buses that can be connected together with the connect function. For example:
  connect gl to "NODE_3"
Unlike the powered form of the connect function, the unpowered form always connects the buses simultaneously whether the connect statements are combined or separate. For example:
  connect s to "NODE_1"; i to "NODE_2"
  ! Both nodes connected at once.
  connect s to "NODE_1"
  ! Both nodes connected at once.
  connect i to "NODE_2"
Unlike powered testing, the G bus is automatically connected to ground in unpowered testing.
