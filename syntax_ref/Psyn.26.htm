power (VCL)
Title: DECLARE POWER‑NODES
The power function appears in the Declaration section of the test to indicate which groups of device pins are connected to power nodes on the PC board.
Power nodes are nodes that are permanently set to some voltage level; this includes ground, the power supplies that power the ICs, and any other node connected to some fixed level.
The power function is required in library tests but is optional in executable tests. The power statements must include all of the power nodes in the logic families (see the family function) specified in the test. The power nodes for the standard families are:
• for TTL – VCC and GND
• for CMOS – VDD and VSS
• for ECL – VCC1, VCC2 and VEE (Mux systems only)
You can change these names in individual board tests if you wish to.
Syntax
  power <group id>
  power <group id>,<group id>
  power <group id>,<group id>, . . . . <group id>
     <group id> is a <label>
Parameters
<group id>
The identifier of a group of pins to be declared as power pins (i.e., connected to a power node). The group must have been defined in an assign to function.
Group <id>’s assigned to power nodes are global throughout the test for a PC board; a power node in one device test cannot be driven or received in the test for another device on the same board.
Example
  assign  VCC  to pins  14, 22
  assign  GND  to pins  7
  assign  Data_1 to pins 3,4,5,8
     . . .
  family TTL
     . . .
  power VCC, GND
  inputs Data_1
     . . .
General Information
Power nodes cannot be driven or received by the vectors, and they cannot be referenced by the inputs, outputs, or bidirectional statements. However, the program generators assign driver and receiver pins to power nodes, for shorts testing and for check probing.
When the test is compiled, the compiler checks to ensure that no attempt has been made to set a logic value on any pin included in a power group.
