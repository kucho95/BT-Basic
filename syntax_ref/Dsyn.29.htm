disable with (VCL)
Title: DISABLE BUSABLE OR UPSTREAM DEVICE
The disable with function is used in the library tests for devices that may have to be disabled while another device is being tested. Disabling implies setting the outputs of the device to their high impedance states.
The disable with statements contain the information which enables the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software to create the disabling statements. The software then places the disabling statements in the executable test for the device under test. The disable with statements are ignored by the software when it creates the executable version of the library test in which the disable with statements reside.
The disable with function specifies the group(s) of pins to be disabled, the group to be used to disable them, and the disabling states to be used to disable the device. If there are many groups of pins, there can be more than one disable with function.
There are two kinds of disabling: single‑vector disabling and multi‑vector disabling. With single‑vector disabling, the disable with statements are placed in the Declaration section of the library test. In this case, the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software gathers all of the disabling states for all devices to be disabled and generates a single vector, DisableVector, that is executed first when the test is run.
If the device can be disabled by just one vector repeated several times, use the optional times parameter. The i3070 In-Circuit Test Software then writes the required number of execution statements or places the execute function in a repeat loop.
With multi‑vector disabling, the disable with statements are placed in a special unit, the unit disable method, in the Vector Execution section of the library test. In this case, the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software reads the vectors associated with the unit and places them in a subroutine, DisableSub, in the executable test. The subroutine is then executed first when the test runs.
When a VCL test that requires other devices to be disabled is run, the disabling vectors are executed first. In the case of the single‑vector disabling, the disabling states are then held, by default, by the remaining vectors in the test, to maintain the disabling.
Multi‑vector disabling optionally uses hold states specified in the disable with statements. After disabling, hold states are held to maintain the disabling for the remainder of the test. Disabling pins that are not specified as hold pins go to their default states but are available for testing purposes if required.
Syntax
  disable <group list 1>
  disable <group list 1> with <group list 2>
  disable <group list 1> with <group list 2> to <drive states>
  disable <group list 1> with <group list 2> to <drive states> <number of> times
  disable <group list 1> with <group list 2> hold <hold states>
     <group list n> can be:   <group id>
                              <group id>,<group id>
                              <group id>,<group id>, . . . <group id>
        <group id> is a <label>
        <drive states> and <hold states> are <string constants>
        <number of> is a <numeric constant>
Parameters
<group list 1>
The groups of pins that are to be disabled. The groups must be in binary format.
<group list 2>
The groups of pins that are to be used to do the disabling. The groups must be in binary format.
<group id>
The identifiers of the pin groups.
<drive states>
(Used in single‑vector disables) states that do the disabling. States allowed: 0 1 T
In the Declaration section of the test, if the states are omitted, the function is ignored by the software's preconditioning routine. However, an error occurs if invalid group names are used.
<hold states>
(Used in a unit disable method) states that must be held for the duration of the test to maintain the disabled states. States allowed: 0 1 K T
<number of>
The number of times that the specified states are to be applied to the disabling pins before they are held. This parameter is a positive integer less than or equal to 65536. If the value is omitted, it defaults to 1 (i.e., apply the states once only).
Examples
(1) ! in the Declaration section
           . . . .
        assign Databus to pins 3, 4, 5, 6
        assign G_ab to pins 1
           . . . .
        inputs G_ab
        bidirectional Databus
        family TTL
           . . . .
        disable Databus with G_ab to "1"     ! applied once and held
           . . . .
(2) ! apply five clock cycles as part of the disable
        disable Register with Clk to "T" 10 times
(3) ! a multi‑vector disable
      ! in the Declaration section
           . . .
        assign Test to pins 1
        assign Clk to pins 2
        assign Reset to pins 3
        assign Out to pins 4
           . . .
        inputs Clk, Test, Reset
        outputs Out
        family TTL on Clk, Test, Reset
           . . .
        pcf order ASIC_Disable is Test, Clk, Reset
   ! in the Vector Execution section
        unit disable method "Disable Out"
           disable Out with Reset hold "0"
           disable Out with Clk hold "T"
           disable Out with Test
           pcf
              use pcf order ASIC_Disable
              "000"
              "010"
              . . . . .
              . . . . .
              "000"
           end pcf
        end unit
General Information
You can include more than one method of disabling a group in your test. This helps the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software decide how to disable the group if there is a topology conflict on the board. However, if the test has too many options, the test generation time may become unacceptably long.
