wait (VCL)
Title: WAIT FOR A SPECIFIED TIME
The wait function is used in the Vector Execution section of a VCL test to pause the test for a specified time. During the wait, the driver states from the preceding vector are held on the inputs to the device under test. At the end of the wait, the test resumes at the next function after the wait function.
The minimum possible length of a wait is related to the internal timing in the system.
In tests with timing sets (on Mux systems only), the minimum time is the event spacing multiplied by the depth of the pipe in the targeted system (event spacing is specified in the events every function). Specify a wait time that is a multiple of the event spacing; if you do not, the time will automatically be rounded up to meet that requirement (note that the actual time will never be less than your specified time).
For tests that do not use timing sets, if you require the minimum wait time, we recommend that you specify a time at least equal to the vector cycle time multipled by the depth of the pipe in the targeted system. If necessary, when the test is compiled, the time will be rounded up to an acceptable value. If you do require the minimum possible wait time for your test, specify too low a value (e.g., 10 ns) and compile the test. The compiler will set the time to the lowest acceptable value and will issue a warning stating what that value is.
It is not practical to predict the maximum time that can be specified for a timed wait; that time depends on the availability and usage of the sequence RAM resources in the system. Theoretically, the wait could last for several hours.
Since the drivers are on during the wait, the wait time is included in the total test time calculated by the SAFEGUARD safety analysis routines.
The machine vectors generated for the wait will require minimum sequencer RAM resources. These vectors will not be counted as user vectors. However, one pipe depth of machine vectors will be counted and, if requested, displayed by the debug software.
Syntax
  wait <seconds>
     <seconds> is a <numeric constant>
Parameters
 
<seconds>
The duration of the wait.
Example
  ! Vector Execution Section
        . . . .
     unit "Multiple waits"
        execute V1
        wait 600n    ! holds driven states from V1
        execute V2
        pcf
           . . .
        end pcf
        wait 1.2u    ! holds drive states from the last PCF vector
        execute V2
           . . .
