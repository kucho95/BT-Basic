condition with (VCL)
Title: CONDITION AN UPSTREAM DEVICE
The condition with function is used in the library tests for devices that may have to be conditioned while another device is being tested. Conditioning implies setting an output, or the outputs, of a device to some known state. If conditioning and disabling is turned on for a test, the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software attempts to disable any devices first and conditions only those devices that it cannot disable.
The condition with function specifies the groups of pins to be conditioned and the states to which they are to be conditioned. The function also, optionally, specifies the groups of pins that are to be driven to do the conditioning, and the states to be driven to those pins. If there are many groups of pins to be conditioned, there can be more than one condition with function. A condition with function is ignored when the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software creates the executable version of the test in which that function appears.
There are two kinds of conditioning: single‑vector conditioning and multi‑vector conditioning. With single‑vector conditioning, the condition with statements are placed in the Declaration section of the library test. In this case, the software gathers all of the conditioning and disabling states for all devices to be preconditioned and generates a single vector, DisableVector, that is executed first when the test is run.
If the device can be conditioned by just one vector repeated several times, use the optional times parameter. The software then writes the required number of execution statements or places the execute function in a repeat loop.
With multi‑vector conditioning, the condition with statements are placed in a special unit, the unit disable method, in the Vector Execution section of the library test. In this case, the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software reads the vectors associated with the unit and places them in a subroutine, DisableSub, in the executable test. The subroutine is then executed first when the test runs.
When a VCL test that requires other devices to be conditioned is run, the conditioning vectors are executed first. In the case of the single‑vector conditioning, the conditioning states are then held, by default, by the remaining vectors in the test, to maintain the conditioning.
Multi‑vector conditioning optionally uses hold states specified in the condition with statements. After conditioning, hold states are held to maintain the conditioning for the remainder of the test. Conditioning pins that are not specified as hold pins go to their default states but are available for testing purposes if required.
Syntax
  condition <group list 1> to <states>
  condition <group list 1> to <states> with <condition spec>
     <group list> can be:   <group id>
                            <group id>, <group id>
                            <group id>, <group id> . . . <group id>
        <group id> is a <label>
     <states> is a <string constant>
     <condition spec> can be:   <group list 2> to <drive states>
                                <group list 2> to <drive states> <number of> times
                                <group list 2> hold <hold states>
        <drive states> and <hold states> are <string constants>
        <number of> is a <numeric constant>
Parameters
<group list 1>
The groups of pins that are to be conditioned. The groups must be in binary format.
<states>
The states to which the conditioned pins are to be set. Only 1’s and 0’s can be specified.
<group list 2>
The groups of pins to be driven to do the conditioning. The groups must be in binary format.
<drive states>
A string of characters indicating the states to be driven to the conditioning pins. States allowed: 1 0 T
In the Declaration section of the test, if the states are omitted, the function is ignored by the software's preconditioning routine. An error occurs if invalid group names are used.
<number of>
(Used with single‑vector conditioning) the number of times that the specified states are to be applied to the conditioning pins before they are held. This parameter is a positive integer less than or equal to 65536. If the value is omitted, it defaults to 1 (i.e., apply the states once only).
<hold states>
(Used in a unit disable method) states that must be held for the duration of the test to maintain the conditioned states. States allowed: 0 1 K T
Examples
(1) ! in the Declaration section
            . . . .
         assign Databus to pins 3, 4, 5, 6
         assign G_ab to pins 1
            . . . .
         inputs G_ab
         bidirectional Databus
         family TTL
            . . . .
         condition Databus to "1111" with G_ab to "1"     ! applied once and held
            . . . .
(2) ! apply five clock cycles as part of the condition
         condition Register to "0" with Clk to "T" 10 times
(3) ! a multi‑vector condition
      ! in the Declaration section
            . . .
         assign Test to pins 1
         assign Clk to pins 2
         assign Reset to pins 3
         assign Out to pins 4
            . . .
         inputs Clk, Test, Reset
         outputs Out
         family TTL on Clk, Test, Reset
            . . .
         pcf order Condition_ASIC is Test, Clk, Reset
      ! in the Vector Execution section
        unit disable method "Condition Out"
           condition Out to "0" with Reset hold "0"
           condition Out to "0" with Clk hold "T"
           condition Out to "0" with Test to "1"
           pcf
           use pcf order Condition_ASIC
           "000"
           "010"
           . . . . .
           "000"
          end pcf
       end unit
General Information
You can include more than one method of conditioning for a group in your test. This helps the i3070 In-Circuit Test Software decide how to condition the group if there is a topology conflict on the board. However, if the test has too many options, the test generation time may become unacceptably long.
